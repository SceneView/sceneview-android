- **AR sessions leave a trace of where tracking stood, and the demo reports a session that never tracks ([#4510](https://github.com/sceneview/sceneview/issues/4510)).** Diagnostics only: they make an AR session whose tracking stays stuck — a sample that sits on "Getting ready" over a live camera image — readable after the fact, and nothing changes on screen. `ARSceneView` now writes one `AR tracking: state=… reason=…` logcat line per tracking transition (tag `SceneView`), including the camera that sits in `PAUSED` with reason `NONE`, which no `onTrackingFailureChanged` reports, and repeats it every ten seconds while the camera is not tracking, with how long that state has lasted (`for=20s`) — which tells a camera that never tracked from one that lost tracking. A throw from the app's own `onSessionUpdated` or `onTrackingFailureChanged` is logged under that callback's name instead of as `ARCore session update failed`, and no longer skips the other callback; `onSessionFailed` / `onSessionFailure` fire exactly when they did. The Android demo files three things with its crash reports when collection is on, each once per AR session: the exception behind an AR failure it has no code for, a session that received camera frames for 10 s without ever tracking (camera state, ARCore's reason, ARCore version), and a resumed session that delivered no frame for 10 s. Its in-app bug report now carries those `AR tracking:` lines in a block of their own ahead of the general log (the last 60 in the shared text, the last 12 in a pre-filled issue, taken out of the issue's existing 400-line ceiling): the general log is the last 1200 lines of the app's process, about twenty seconds of an AR session, which the line that matters has usually left.
